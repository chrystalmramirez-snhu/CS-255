# CS-255
The DriverPass project was a system design project for a driving school that wanted a way to manage its customers, driving lessons, instructors, appointments, packages, and online practice materials. I worked through the client's requirements and designed the system around the different people who would use it, including customers, secretaries, drivers/instructors, the owner or manager, and the IT administrator. I think one thing I did particularly well was learning to look at the system from each user's perspective instead of only thinking about what the system needed to do. As I worked on my UML diagrams, I became more aware of how important it was to make sure the right users were connected to the right functions. I also think I improved at explaining my design to someone who may not have a technical background. For example, when working on the security and limitations portions of the presentation, I had to think about how I could explain my decisions in a way that would make sense to the business owner rather than using technical terms. 

If I could go back and revise one part of the project, I would probably spend more time organizing and simplifying my UML diagrams. While working on the project, I noticed that it was possible for a diagram to be technically correct but still be difficult to follow when there were too many connections. Looking at my classmates' diagrams also helped me recognize different ways information could be organized and made me more aware of clarity and layout. Overall, this project changed the way I think about software design. I would start by listening to the client and identifying what each user actually needs to accomplish, then use tools such as use case, activity, sequence, and class diagrams to organize those requirements into a design. I learned that designing software can be complex but needs to be practical for the people who will be using it. In future projects, I would continue moving in purposeful steps and revising as needed after thinking through each process.
